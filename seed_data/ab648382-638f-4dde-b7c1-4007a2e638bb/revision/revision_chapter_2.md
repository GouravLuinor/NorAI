# Revision Notes — Chapter 2: Hierarchical Feature Learning and Abstraction

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Neural networks use hierarchical layers to transform raw pixel data into abstract features.
- Neuron activation is calculated as a weighted sum, where weights act as spatial filters.
- Early layers detect simple features like edges, while deeper layers combine these into complex shapes.
- The final classification is determined by the output neuron with the highest activation value.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Forward Propagation**: The process of passing input data through layers of a neural network to compute an output.
- **Hierarchical Abstraction**: The strategy of building complex representations by composing simpler features across multiple layers.
- **Weighted Sum**: The mathematical operation where inputs are multiplied by weights to determine a neuron's activation level.
