# Revision Notes — Chapter 9: Solving Physics Problems

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Always define your coordinate system (origin and positive direction) clearly before starting calculations.
- Identify the initial conditions ($y_0, v_0, a$) to construct the specific position equation for the object.
- Recognize that kinematic formulas are only valid under the assumptions used to derive them, such as constant acceleration.
- Use physical constraints (e.g., velocity is zero at maximum height, position is zero at ground impact) to solve for unknown variables.
- Interpret mathematical solutions (like negative time) within the context of the physical scenario.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Initial Conditions**: The starting values of position and velocity at time zero that define the specific motion of an object.
- **Falling Objects**: Objects moving under the influence of gravity, modeled with constant acceleration in kinematic equations.
- **Interpreting Solutions**: The process of evaluating mathematical results to ensure they make physical sense within the constraints of the problem.
- **Problem-solving Strategy**: A systematic approach involving defining coordinates, identifying assumptions, and applying physical constraints to solve for unknowns.
