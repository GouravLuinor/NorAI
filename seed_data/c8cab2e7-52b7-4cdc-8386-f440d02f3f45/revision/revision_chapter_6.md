# Revision Notes — Chapter 6: Velocity, Acceleration, and Calculus

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Position-time graphs show $x(t)$, not the physical path of an object.
- Average velocity $\bar{v}$ depends only on endpoints and can be zero even if the object moved.
- Instantaneous velocity is the derivative $\frac{dx}{dt}$, representing the slope of the tangent line to the $x(t)$ curve.
- Acceleration is the second derivative of position, $\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$, and is often treated as a constant in basic kinematics.
- The third derivative of position has no standard physical name.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Position-time graph**: A plot of an object's position as a function of time, used to visualize motion.
- **Average velocity**: The total displacement divided by the total time elapsed between two points.
- **Instantaneous velocity**: The velocity at a specific moment, calculated as the derivative of position with respect to time.
- **Acceleration**: The rate of change of velocity, calculated as the derivative of velocity or the second derivative of position.
