# Revision Notes — Chapter 7: Constant Acceleration and Integration

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Integration is the inverse of differentiation; it involves guessing a function whose derivatives match the given acceleration.
- The general position function for constant acceleration is $X(t) = \frac{1}{2}AT^2 + BT + C$.
- The constant $C$ represents the initial position ($X_0$) at $T=0$.
- The constant $B$ represents the initial velocity ($V_0$) at $T=0$.
- The final kinematic equation is $X(t) = X_0 + V_0T + \frac{1}{2}AT^2$.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Constant Acceleration**: A state of motion where the acceleration of an object does not change over time.
- **Integration**: The mathematical process of finding a function given its derivative, essentially working backwards from acceleration to position.
- **Initial Position**: The location of an object at time $T=0$, represented by the constant $C$ in the integrated position function.
- **Initial Velocity**: The velocity of an object at time $T=0$, represented by the constant $B$ in the integrated velocity function.
