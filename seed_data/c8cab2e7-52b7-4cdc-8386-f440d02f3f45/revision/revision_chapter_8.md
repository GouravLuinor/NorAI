# Revision Notes — Chapter 8: Kinematic Equations and Applications

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Constant acceleration motion is defined by a quadratic position equation: $x(t) = x_0 + v_0t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.
- The time-independent velocity formula $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)$ is derived by substituting $t = (v - v_0)/a$ into the position equation.
- Initial conditions ($x_0$ and $v_0$) are essential for determining the future trajectory of an object.
- Understanding the calculus-based derivation of these formulas reduces the need for rote memorization.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Kinematic equations**: Mathematical expressions that describe the position and velocity of an object under constant acceleration.
- **Time-independent velocity formula**: A derived equation that relates velocity and displacement without requiring the explicit value of time.
- **Eliminating time**: The algebraic process of substituting the time variable from the velocity equation into the position equation to create a new relationship.
