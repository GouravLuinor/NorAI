# Revision Notes — Chapter 6: Risk Management, Insurance, and Portfolio Theory

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Financial technology evolves alongside information technology, making previously uneconomical inventions viable.
- Insurance emerged in the 1600s following the development of probability theory.
- Portfolio management pools risk through diversification or negative correlation, utilizing models like CAPM.
- The Efficient Markets Hypothesis posits that markets incorporate information rapidly, with prediction markets serving as a practical example of this efficiency.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Risk Pooling**: The practice of aggregating individual risks, such as life or fire insurance, to manage them collectively.
- **Portfolio Diversification**: Assembling a collection of assets that are diversified or negatively correlated to manage risk exposure.
- **Efficient Markets Hypothesis**: The theory that financial markets incorporate new information rapidly, leading to accurate asset pricing.
- **Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)**: A theoretical model used in portfolio management to describe the relationship between systematic risk and expected return.
