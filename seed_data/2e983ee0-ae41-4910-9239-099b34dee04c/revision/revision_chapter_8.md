# Revision Notes — Chapter 8: Debt Markets, Stocks, and Real Estate

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Interest rates, the price of time, fluctuate based on maturity and can be negative in real terms during aggressive monetary policy.
- Stock valuation is complicated by management decisions on leverage (debt) and dividend payouts, as well as share repurchases.
- The U.S. housing market experienced a massive 85% real price increase from 1997 to 2006 before beginning a decline.
- Housing market instability is a global phenomenon, often linked to speculative behavior and the mortgage market.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Term Structure of Interest Rates**: The theory explaining how interest rates vary according to the maturity or term of the debt instrument.
- **Real Interest Rates**: Interest rates adjusted for inflation, often determined by instruments indexed to the price level.
- **Stock Leverage**: The amount of debt a company takes on, which influences the risk and valuation of its stock.
- **Speculative Housing Market**: A market condition where home prices rise rapidly and become unstable, often followed by a correction or bust.
