# Revision Notes — Chapter 10: Brokerages, Futures, Options, and Democratization

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Information technology has enabled the rapid creation of electronic exchanges, fundamentally changing the brokerage industry.
- Futures contracts are standardized exchange-traded instruments, while forward contracts are customized over-the-counter agreements.
- Futures markets provide insights into future price expectations, though they should be interpreted carefully using economic theory.
- Options provide the right to buy assets and are essential tools for managing financial risk.
- The democratization of finance seeks to broaden access to financial services but risks exposing less knowledgeable participants to exploitation.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Brokerages**: Financial intermediaries that are currently being transformed by rapid advancements in information technology.
- **Futures Markets**: Exchanges where standardized contracts for future delivery are traded, providing signals about future price expectations.
- **Options Markets**: Platforms where contracts granting the right to buy assets are traded, serving as critical tools for risk management.
- **Democratization of Finance**: The process of making sophisticated financial services accessible to the general population, which carries both benefits and risks.
