# Revision Notes — Chapter 5: Guest Speakers and Course Outline

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Philanthropy is presented as the ideal outcome for successful individuals in financial markets.
- The Yale endowment, managed by David Swenson, grew from under $1 billion to over $22 billion through consistent, high-performing investment strategies.
- Andrew Redleaf is highlighted for his independent thinking and his ability to foresee and profit from the subprime crisis.
- The course will focus on markets and institutions, beginning with the principle of pooling and hedging risk as the foundation of insurance.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **David Swenson**: The manager of the Yale endowment who achieved significant growth through consistent, high-performing investment strategies.
- **Yale Endowment**: The collection of financial assets owned by Yale University, excluding non-liquid assets like art and buildings.
- **Andrew Redleaf**: A hedge fund manager known for independent thinking and correctly predicting the subprime mortgage crisis.
- **Risk Pooling**: The principle of spreading individual risks across a larger group to reduce the impact on any single person.
