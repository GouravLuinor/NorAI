# Revision Notes — Chapter 7: Efficient Markets, Behavioral Finance, and Regulation

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Markets are not perfectly efficient; human psychology plays a major role in driving market behavior.
- Excess returns are possible for those who understand human nature and its interaction with institutions.
- Regulation is critical for market success, involving both government oversight and self-regulatory organizations like FINRA.
- Debt markets are essentially markets for time, ranging from overnight federal funds rates to infinite-maturity perpetuities.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Efficient Markets Hypothesis**: A theory suggesting markets are perfectly efficient, making it impossible to earn excess returns.
- **Behavioral Finance**: An approach that incorporates human psychology to explain market behavior, challenging the assumption of perfect efficiency.
- **Self-Regulatory Organizations (SROs)**: Industry-created organizations, such as FINRA, that establish and enforce rules for their members.
- **Debt Markets**: Markets for financial instruments based on promises to pay, representing a market for time.
