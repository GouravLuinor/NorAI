# Revision Notes — Chapter 3: The Distortions of Price Controls

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Prices act as essential information signals; suppressing them creates market disequilibrium.
- Price ceilings set below equilibrium cause shortages by increasing quantity demanded while decreasing quantity supplied.
- Rent control leads to unintended consequences: reduced housing quality, property abandonment, and inefficient allocation of space.
- When price signals are removed, rationing shifts from price-based to non-price-based mechanisms, often resulting in waste and shortages.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Price Ceiling**: A government-imposed maximum price set below the market equilibrium, resulting in shortages.
- **Market Equilibrium**: The price point where the quantity supplied equals the quantity demanded.
- **Shortage**: A condition where quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price.
- **Negative Externalities**: Unintended harmful side effects of a policy, such as building degradation under rent control.
