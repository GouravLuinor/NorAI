# Revision Notes — Chapter 2: Price Signals and Market Coordination

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative foregone; it includes the lost potential of compounding returns.
- Wealth is determined by the efficiency of resource allocation, not just the possession of natural resources.
- Prices serve as decentralized signals that coordinate supply and demand without the need for central planning.
- Centralized planning fails in complex economies because it cannot process the vast, dynamic information that price signals aggregate automatically.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Opportunity Cost**: The value of the next best alternative that must be sacrificed when a choice is made.
- **Price Signals**: Market-determined prices that convey information about scarcity and demand to producers and consumers.
- **Spontaneous Order**: The emergence of organized systems from the decentralized actions of individuals, without central direction.
- **Information Asymmetry**: A situation where different parties have different information; markets use prices to bridge this gap.
