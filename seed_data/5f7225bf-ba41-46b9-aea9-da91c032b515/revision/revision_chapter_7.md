# Revision Notes — Chapter 7: The Founders, Virtue, and Constitutional Design

## Key Exam Takeaways

- The founders were deeply influenced by classical moral philosophers like Cicero and Aristotle.
- Constitutional design, including checks and balances and an independent judiciary, was intended to contain human passions and factions.
- Madison argued that a constitution must both select virtuous leaders and create structural precautions to keep them virtuous.
- Hamilton questioned whether human societies could establish government through 'reflection and choice' rather than 'accident and force'.
- The U.S. Constitution has been continuously contested and amended, with the Civil War serving as the most significant transformative event.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Founding Fathers**: A group of complex, often contradictory individuals who were deeply influenced by classical and enlightenment moral philosophy.
- **Federalist Papers**: A series of writings by Hamilton and Madison that articulated the rationale for constitutional design, specifically regarding factions and human nature.
- **Constitutional Checks and Balances**: Structural mechanisms, including an independent judiciary and a powerful executive, designed to contain human passions and prevent abuse of power.
- **Political Virtue**: The quality of leaders who possess the wisdom to discern and the commitment to pursue the common good of society.
