# Revision Notes — Chapter 9: Racial Reckoning and the Social Contract

## Key Exam Takeaways

- A 'racial reckoning' involves a collective effort to define the nation's path, though current engagement may be limited.
- The American social contract is tested by its ability to uphold 14th Amendment promises and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.
- The 1860-61 period serves as the primary historical benchmark for a failed social contract leading to Civil War.
- Decreasing confidence in shared institutions is a key parallel between 1860 and modern political divisions.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Racial Reckoning**: A process of collective societal reflection and decision-making regarding the nation's direction and its commitment to equality.
- **Social Contract**: The implicit agreement that binds citizens together, based on shared legal foundations like the 14th Amendment and the Voting Rights Act.
- **Political Division**: The state of being split by cultural and institutional disagreements, which, if left unresolved, threatens the unity of the nation.
