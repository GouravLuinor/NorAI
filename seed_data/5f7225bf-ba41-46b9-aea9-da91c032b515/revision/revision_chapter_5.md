# Revision Notes — Chapter 5: Yale's Founders and the Reinterpretation of History

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Universities historically suppressed uncomfortable history through a culture of moderate caution.
- Seven of Yale's ten clerical founders were slaveholders, with the namesake of Woodbridge Hall being the largest among them.
- The lecturer advocates for reinterpreting historical landmarks rather than removing them.
- A proposed solution is to repurpose buildings like Woodbridge Hall into museums that center the history of slavery alongside other institutional achievements.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Institutional Memory**: The process by which universities choose to acknowledge, suppress, or reinterpret their past actions and historical figures.
- **Reinterpretation**: The practice of keeping historical landmarks and names in place while providing new context and education to address their connection to slavery.
