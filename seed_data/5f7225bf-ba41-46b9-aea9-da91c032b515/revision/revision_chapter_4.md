# Revision Notes — Chapter 4: Can It Happen Here? Authoritarianism and Modern Challenges

## Key Exam Takeaways

- Sinclair Lewis's 'It Can't Happen Here' serves as a framework for analyzing potential threats to modern democracy.
- Modern slavery persists globally in forms like labor and sex trafficking, with numbers potentially exceeding those of the Civil War era.
- The 'shock of events' refers to unforeseen historical moments like 9/11 or pandemics that force societies to change their political positions.
- Contemporary concerns include evolving forms of voter suppression and public anxiety regarding the possibility of a new, dystopian civil war.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **It Can't Happen Here**: A 1935 novel by Sinclair Lewis depicting a fascist takeover of the US, used here to question the stability of modern democracy.
- **Shock of events**: Unforeseen historical occurrences that disrupt social and political systems, forcing a reevaluation of societal positions.
- **Modern bondage**: Contemporary forms of enslavement, such as human and labor trafficking, which exist in global supply chains.
