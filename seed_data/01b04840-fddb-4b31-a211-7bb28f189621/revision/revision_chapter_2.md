# Revision Notes — Chapter 2: Defining the Tangent Line

## Key Exam Takeaways

- A line through point $(x_0, y_0)$ is defined by $y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)$.
- The derivative $f'(x_0)$ is defined as the slope of the tangent line to $y = f(x)$ at $P(x_0, f(x_0))$.
- A tangent line is the limit of secant lines $PQ$ as the point $Q$ approaches the fixed point $P$.
- The slope of the tangent line is the limit of the slopes of the secant lines as the distance between points vanishes.

## Core Concepts Breakdown

- **Tangent line equation**: The linear equation $y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)$ used to describe the tangent line at a specific point.
- **Slope**: The value $m$ in the line equation, which for a tangent line is equal to the derivative $f'(x_0)$.
- **Secant line**: A line intersecting a curve at two distinct points, $P$ and $Q$, used to approximate the tangent line.
- **Limit**: The mathematical process where the secant line $PQ$ approaches the tangent line as $Q$ moves closer to $P$.
